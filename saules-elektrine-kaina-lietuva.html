How Much Does a Solar System Cost in Lithuania in 2026, and Is It Worth Investing?

July 6, 2026 8 min read Sunlytics team

In 2026, the solar market in Lithuania is more mature than ever. Prices have dropped nearly 40% over the past five years, while electricity tariffs keep rising. That's made solar one of the best private investments available in Lithuania. Still, many people don't know where to start or what the real price is, as opposed to a marketing brochure figure.

This article is for anyone seriously considering a solar system who wants to understand the numbers before talking to installers.

Key factors that determine a solar system's price

Before getting into specific numbers, it's worth understanding what actually drives the price. In our experience, two properties with similar roofs can end up with very different prices for a few reasons:

  • System power (kWp): the larger the system, the lower the price per kilowatt.
  • Roof type: flat roofs are usually more expensive to install on than pitched roofs, due to the need for special mounting structures.
  • Roof orientation and shading: a south-facing roof with no shade is ideal. East- or west-facing roofs reduce efficiency by 15-25%.
  • Distance and infrastructure: the further the inverter is from the electrical panel, the more cabling and labor cost.
  • Inverter type: string inverters are cheaper, but modular options (microinverters or optimizers) work better on shaded roofs.

Will a solar system pay off in Lithuania?

Short answer: yes, and payback periods improve every year for two reasons: solar equipment prices keep falling, while electricity tariffs keep rising.

Let's compare solar with other investments:

  • Bank deposit in 2026: 2-3% annual return
  • Rental apartment in Vilnius: 5-7% annual return
  • Solar system: 10-20% annual return (depending on the property)

A solar system also increases property value. Studies across Europe show that homes with solar panels sell for 4-6% more on average.

Commercial properties: why it's an even better investment

In a residential home, most of the electricity generated by a solar system gets used in the evening, at night, or in winter, when the system isn't producing. That means a large share of the energy gets exported to the grid at a lower rate.

In a commercial building, it's the opposite: electricity gets used during the day, exactly when the system produces the most. That means almost all the generated energy is consumed on-site immediately, and payback can be as short as 3-5 years.

Real-world example: a warehouse or production facility with a 50 kWp system, operating weekdays 8am-6pm, typically pays back in 4-5 years and generates €150,000-€250,000 in net profit over 25 years, depending on consumption.

Common mistakes when choosing a solar system

  • Choosing based on price alone. The cheapest system often uses lower-quality panels that produce 15-20% less electricity after 10 years. Think in terms of a 25-year outlook.
  • Skipping a roof analysis before buying. Shade from a chimney or a neighboring house can cut system efficiency by 20-40%. It's important to know this before investing.
  • Forgetting about maintenance. Solar panels need minimal upkeep (cleaning once a year), but inverters can occasionally fail and need repair. Negotiate the warranty terms.
  • Not coordinating with ESO early. Grid connection can take 2-6 months. Start this process as early as possible.
